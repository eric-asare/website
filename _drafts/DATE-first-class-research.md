I have been wondering what it means to do first-class research.
I mean important research. Well what is important research?
Reading Hamming (link) and Anannnaa (link) makes me think it is really how you do it. Yes what you do counts.
You want to be working on a problem people care about (people usually mean scientific - to be more specific the software engineering community and often the industry care about but I think we should also consider _the user of software that is you and me and the neighbour nextdoor who might not be scientific at all or work in the software industry_. This last part I will need to think more about in another blog post- How do we choose problems that not only the scientific community and the industry care about but your neighbour next door - perhaps interdisciplinary research has something to bear on this).
Ok I went on a tangent, where am I going with all this; your research project (not problem - more on this in this link) should not be far removed from reality.

Now that you are working on something that is hot or at least someone cares about. How do you work on it?

1. Reproducable and I mean really reproducable
   In a way that others can stand on your shoulders not your feet (quote from Hamming).
   Research moves fast and often our cold is tightly coupled with our setup and environment and hardly reproducible (example is if you run on HPC). With GenAI advancement in coding task, it shouldn't take us more than an hour to restructure our code for replication once the paper is done and submitted for review (yet to try though). This way someone wanting to build upon your work does not have to start from scratch.

2. Accessible (Researchable)
   - Write a takeaway on your website somewhere so that someone looking at your website might know at a glance why this was important/ or not to do. Abstracts and titles can get too technical and obscure.
   - Share a post on linkedin (or whatever social media you use) about why some people should care
   - If you give a talk, record it or at least post the slides.
